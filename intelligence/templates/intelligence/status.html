The valuation

Choosing the comparables

Candidates are drawn in three passes: the same property type in the same city, then the same state, then anywhere. Each pass that has to be reached costs the comparables relevance, and the code applies that cost explicitly rather than pretending a flat in another state is as informative as one next door.

Scoring each one

Size carries the most weight, at 34%, because a price per square foot is only meaningful between properties of a similar size. Location follows at 26%, then how recently the comparable was listed at 15%, configuration at 14%, and age at 11%. Anything scoring below 0.25 is discarded.

Rebasing across cities

A comparable from another city is rebased onto the target city's median rate before its price per square foot is used. Without that step a one bedroom flat in Bandra gets compared against a flat in Kothrud and comes out at a third of its value. The index is derived from the listings on the site, not from a hard-coded table.

Blending and trimming

The blended rate is a similarity-weighted mean. When there are five or more comparables, any whose rate sits more than two standard deviations from the median is dropped first, so one mispriced listing cannot move the result.

Adjustments

The blended rate is then adjusted for the ways this property differs from the set: age, furnishing, amenity count, aspect, floor position, title type and carpet ratio. Only differences that were actually observed produce an adjustment, so the list on a listing page is never padded out to look thorough.

The range, and confidence

The interval widens with the scatter of the comparable rates and narrows with the square root of how many there are. High confidence needs five or more comparables and a spread under 12%. Below three comparables the method falls back to a regional benchmark and says so on the page.

The risk score

Seven factors, one hundred points

Documents missing carries 24 points, seller identity 20, price against the valuation 16, failed document checks 14, listing completeness 10, title type 10, and time on the market 6. Under 20 is low risk, under 40 moderate, under 65 elevated, and above that high. Every factor that contributed appears on the listing with its points and its reason.

What it does not do

It does not predict fraud, and it does not know anything that is not in the record. A clean score means the checkable things check out, not that the property is a good buy.

The language model layer

What it is allowed to do

Four things: explain a valuation it has been handed, draft listing copy from the structured fields, answer a question using only that listing's record, and turn a plain-language search into filters that are then validated against the same allow-list the search form uses.

What happens when it is not there

Each of those four has a local implementation that runs instead. The explanation is assembled from the comparables and adjustments, the copy is built from the fields, the answer is matched against the record, and the search is parsed with rules. Nothing on the site disappears, and every piece of generated text says which layer produced it.

Providers

Groq, Google AI Studio, OpenRouter, and a local Ollama instance. All four have a free tier or no cost at all. One environment variable selects between them, and an unset variable means the local layer answers everything.
