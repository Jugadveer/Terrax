What goes on chain

Not the documents. A property's paperwork stays off chain, because publishing scans of a title deed would be expensive and a privacy failure at the same time. What is recorded is the pair that makes the paperwork checkable: where it is published, and a hash of it.

The two contracts

PropertyDeed, an ERC-721

One token per verified property. It stores an IPFS identifier for the evidence bundle and a keccak256 hash of that bundle. Only the issuer can record a property, because Terrax checks documents and identity before a listing publishes, and an open mint would let anyone record a property nobody checked and inherit the credibility of the ones that were.

A re-valuation calls updateEvidence, which writes a new hash and leaves the previous one in the event log. The history is not rewritten, only extended.

PropertyShares, an ERC-1155

Fractional ownership. The token id is the deed's token id, so a share and the property it is a share of are joined by the number rather than by a lookup table that can drift. The supply is fixed when the pool opens and cannot be raised, because raising it after people have bought in dilutes them silently.

This mirrors the ledger the rest of the site keeps. issue is the primary sale a buyer makes, and redeem is the exit. The invariant the Python tests assert, that issued shares never exceed the supply, is enforced here by the contract rather than by convention.

Checking a listing yourself

Three steps, no special tools

Fetch the bundle from its IPFS identifier. Serialise it with sorted keys and no whitespace. Take the keccak256 of those bytes, and compare it against what the contract holds for that deed.

Change one document, one figure or one word and the hashes stop matching. The verify button on every recorded listing runs exactly this.

What this does not prove

That a document is genuine, that the seller owns the property, or that the valuation is right. It proves only that what you are being shown now is what was recorded then. Everything else is what the document checks, the risk score and the identity review are for, and those are judgements rather than proofs.
